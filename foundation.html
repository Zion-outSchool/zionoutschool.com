Who We Are

ZION outSchool is rooted in the gospel of Jesus Christ and built on the LDS standard works — including the Book of Mormon and the Come Follow Me curriculum. Scripture is not a subject we add on. It is the organizing spine of every unit of study. Theology, history, science, and literature all radiate outward from it.

We welcome families of any faith background who share our conviction that education and spiritual formation are not separate endeavors. You do not need to be a member of the LDS church to enroll your child here. You do need to be comfortable with — and ideally excited by — a learning environment where scriptural truth is taken seriously as an academic and moral foundation.

What We Believe

Five convictions that shape everything we do.

01

Every child carries divine potential.

Potential a standardized education cannot reach, cannot measure, and was not designed to see. We build for what each child is capable of — not what a grade level assumes.

02

All truth is integrated.

The same God who parted the Red Sea also wrote the laws of fluid dynamics. Students who learn to see those connections become genuinely educated people — not just credentialed ones.

03

Children learn to think by being asked to think.

Not by being given answers to memorize. The classroom is a place of questions before it is a place of conclusions. We protect that sequence.

04

Parents are partners, not passengers.

The learning that happens at home matters as much as what happens in this room. We teach with that assumption and we design for it deliberately.

05

Freedom, faith, and family are the goal — not the backdrop.

They are not abstract values printed on a mission statement. They are the destination of everything we do in this school, every day, in every subject.

"Once you learn to read, you will be forever free."
— Frederick Douglass
In the Classroom

Belief becomes practice.

A second grader and an eighth grader sit in the same room, read the same texts, and arrive at the same question — but what each one does with it reflects exactly where they are and how far they can reach from there. That is not accidental. It is the point.

Every session begins and ends with prayer, because we believe truth does not originate with the teacher or the curriculum. It comes through both, toward the child, from a source greater than either. The teacher's role is not to deliver information. It is to create the conditions in which a child can encounter something true — and be changed by it.

What parents see when they visit is children writing in the margins of their books, defending claims with evidence from two texts written thousands of years apart, and asking questions the teacher didn't put there. What we are building, underneath all of it, is a child who knows how to pursue truth — and who has discovered that learning is an act of reverence.
