Our classroom spans Kindergarten through 12th grade by design. Older students deepen their understanding by teaching younger ones. Younger students are pulled forward by the intellectual ceiling of the room. Students work at appropriate levels within a shared community, with teachers guiding instruction and checking understanding.

This is not a limitation of our size. It is one of our greatest strengths. A second grader who watches an eighth grader defend a position under questioning understands, without being told, what serious thinking looks like. An eighth grader who has to explain a concept to a second grader discovers quickly whether they actually understand it.

At ZION, students prepare and lead their own councils, bringing work samples, reflections, interests, and hopes for the future. Parents offer guidance, and teachers listen, take notes, and help shape practical next steps.

The conversation reaches beyond grades and classroom concerns to consider who each student is becoming and how they can grow spiritually, socially, physically, and intellectually. Together, we set meaningful goals and identify the support, mentorships, projects, and experiences that can help students pursue them.

These councils put our mission into practice: honoring parents' stewardship, helping students take responsibility for their education, and partnering to help them discover their God-given gifts and use them to serve. Each council ends with a shared plan and clear commitments from the student, family, and ZION.

Our curriculum is rooted in the LDS standard works, including the Book of Mormon and the Come Follow Me program. Membership in the LDS church is not required for enrollment. Families of any faith background are welcome — you do need to be comfortable with — and ideally excited by — a learning environment where scriptural truth is taken seriously as an academic and moral foundation.

Every session opens and closes with prayer. Spiritual formation is not separated from academic work here. It is woven through it.

We teach from the LDS standard works — the four volumes of scripture recognized by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints:

  • The Bible (King James Version)
  • The Book of Mormon
  • The Doctrine and Covenants
  • The Pearl of Great Price
  • The words of modern day Prophets and Apostles.

Scripture serves as the organizing spine of each unit of study — not as a devotional addition, but as the primary academic text from which science, history, literature, and language arts all radiate outward.

Come Follow Me is a scripture study curriculum developed by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. It moves through the standard works on a rotating annual cycle, providing structured study questions, context, and commentary designed for both individual and family use.

At ZION outSchool, Come Follow Me provides the scriptural sequence that anchors each unit of study. Rather than using it as a devotional program, we build our entire academic curriculum around it — treating each scripture block as the lens through which science, history, literature, and language arts are studied that unit.

You do not need to be familiar with Come Follow Me before enrolling. Students encounter it as part of their academic work here.

This is a question worth answering honestly, because families of other Christian backgrounds deserve to know what they are enrolling their child into.

The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints holds Jesus Christ as its center and Savior — that is the shared foundation with other Christian traditions. Several doctrinal distinctives set it apart:

  • LDS theology holds that God the Father, Jesus Christ, and the Holy Ghost are three distinct beings — not one unified Trinity as defined by the Nicene Creed.
  • The Church believes in continuing revelation — that God speaks through living prophets today, as He did in ancient scripture.
  • The Book of Mormon is accepted as a second witness of Jesus Christ alongside the Bible, not a replacement of it.
  • LDS doctrine teaches that humans are literal spirit children of God with divine potential — a view of human nature that shapes how we think about children and learning.

These beliefs are not hidden or softened here. They are woven into the curriculum because they are the foundation of how we understand truth, knowledge, and the purpose of education. Families who enroll their children here should understand and be at peace with that foundation — even if they do not share every point of doctrine.

If you have questions about LDS beliefs before enrolling, we are happy to talk through them directly. Reach out here.

Accepted or actively enrolling ZION outSchool families may apply for need-based scholarship assistance when EFA funds, family support, or other education funding are not sufficient to cover the student's tuition obligation for the current academic year.

Scholarship decisions are made through an objective, nondiscriminatory review process. Financial need is the primary factor, along with the student's enrollment status, available education funding, the family's sustainable contribution, and the scholarship funds available at the time of review.

Awards are not automatic and are not guaranteed. When awarded, assistance is for one academic year at a time, and families must reapply each year if continued assistance is needed.

The application asks for basic student and parent information, household size, education funding, income range, major financial obligations, limited available assets, possible family or sponsor support, and a short explanation of any circumstances we should understand. Families are also asked to upload the first two pages of last year's tax return. Social Security numbers may be redacted before upload.

Scholarship gifts are held for the scholarship program as a whole. Donors may support the fund, but they may not select or direct an award to a specific student or family.
